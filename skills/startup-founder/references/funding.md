# Funding: Bootstrap vs Raise

## Decision framework
Raise money only if BOTH are true:
1. The opportunity requires spending significantly ahead of revenue (e.g. building a marketplace that needs supply+demand simultaneously, capital-intensive hardware, land-grab network effects where speed beats profitability).
2. You're willing to trade equity + control + a board for that speed.

Bootstrap if:
- You can reach paying customers within weeks/months with a small team.
- The market rewards profitability and steady growth over blitzscaling (most dev tools, agencies, niche SaaS, service businesses).
- You want to keep full control and optionality (can always raise later once there's traction — much better terms).

Nalin's nerdev-style studio/SaaS profile: bootstrapping is the default fit. Raise only if a specific product idea genuinely needs upfront capital to win (rare for solo/small dev-tooling plays).

## If bootstrapping
- Revenue-fund from day one: charge early, even a small amount, to validate willingness to pay.
- Consider services/freelancing (see the `freelancing` skill if available) to fund product development while building the real product on the side.
- Use startup credit programs to cut costs: AWS Activate, Google Cloud for Startups, Microsoft for Startups, MongoDB for Startups, Notion/Segment/Stripe Atlas startup programs — apply for these once incorporated, they cut infra cost close to zero for the first year.

## If raising
### Sources
- Pre-seed/seed: angel investors (personal networks, AngelList, Indian angel networks — Indian Angel Network, LetsVenture, Venture Catalysts), accelerators (Y Combinator, 500 Startups/500 Global, Techstars, and in India: Startup India Seed Fund, various state-level programs).
- YC's own guide (yc/library "A Guide to Seed Fundraising") is the single best free resource — read it before pitching anyone.

### Pitch deck (standard structure, ~10-12 slides)
1. Problem
2. Solution
3. Why now
4. Market size (bottom-up, not just "huge TAM")
5. Product (demo/screenshots)
6. Traction (any signal: users, revenue, LOIs, waitlist)
7. Business model / pricing
8. Go-to-market
9. Competition / why you win
10. Team
11. Financials/ask (how much, for what, runway)
12. Contact/close

### What investors actually check
- Founder-market fit: why *you* are positioned to win this.
- Evidence you've talked to real customers (not just an idea in a vacuum).
- Clear, specific use of funds and milestones the round buys.

## India-specific funding notes
- **Startup India** registration (DPIIT recognition) unlocks tax benefits, easier compliance, and access to the Startup India Seed Fund Scheme — do this once you have Pvt Ltd registration and reasonable traction, not before.
- Indian angel networks: Indian Angel Network, LetsVenture, Venture Catalysts, 100X.VC, Titan Capital.
- Razorpay, MongoDB, AWS, and several others run India-specific startup programs (free credits, discounted tooling) — check current terms since they change (search rather than rely on memory).
- FDI/foreign investment rules matter if raising from outside India — this needs a CA/lawyer, not general advice.
