## 2. Missing-tool list building

Use this when the absence of a product is the real signal.

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Name the category and the tool whose absence gives the game away |
| 2 | List the surrounding tools that show they care (for example: GA, Hotjar, GTM) |
| 3 | Search for companies running the surrounding stack without the target tool |
| 4 | Write the gap as one sentence from their side, not yours |
| 5 | Split the list by maturity or platform, because the pitch changes per segment |
| 6 | Contact the head of growth with a mini-audit observation, not a pitch |

**Segment split example:**

| Stack | Segment | Pitch |
|---|---|---|
| WordPress + GA + Hotjar | Marketing-led company | "Understand which visitors become real users." |
| Shopify + GA + Hotjar | E-commerce brand | "See exactly where buyers drop off between product page and checkout." |

Same gap, different message. Benchmarks from one founder running this play: 31% reply rate, 1 in 5 replies converted to a demo.

**Opening line:**

> Noticed you are using Hotjar and GA but nothing for product events. Curious how you are tracking activation today?

## 3. Customer development question bank

**Current state**

- Walk me through the last time you dealt with [problem]. What happened?
- How do you handle it today? What does it cost you in time, money, or stress?
- What have you tried before, and why did you stop?

**Decision and budget**

- If you fixed this, whose budget would it come from?
- Who else would need to agree?
- What would make this a priority this quarter rather than next year?

**Information and trust (this is where entry points come from)**

- Who do you go to for advice on this? A person, a peer group, a consultant?
- Which newsletters, podcasts, or accounts do you actually read?
- Are there any chats or communities where you talk to peers? Which are genuinely useful?
- Which conferences are worth going to, and which ones are just vendors selling to each other?
- Outside work, what do you do with peers: sports, clubs, dinners?

**Close every call with**

- Who else should I talk to about this? Would you be comfortable introducing me?

Do not pitch. If they ask what you are building, answer in one sentence and turn it back to them.

## 4. Getting the first 10 conversations

- Ask for 20 minutes of *advice*, not a meeting. People say yes to being experts.
- Mine second-degree connections: ask friends, investors, and former colleagues "who do you know who is a [role] at a [company type]?"
- Offer something: a summary of what you learn across all interviews, an introduction, a relevant resource.
- Every interview ends with a referral ask. Ten conversations usually come from 3 to 4 seeds.

## 6. Entry-point scoring

For each candidate place, score 1 to 3 on:

- **Density.** How many ICP-fit people are there?
- **Quality.** Decision-makers, or vendors and juniors?
- **Access.** Can the user get in naturally and honestly?
- **Intimacy.** Can real conversations happen? A padel match beats a 3,000-person expo hall.

Prioritize high intimacy plus decent access, even at lower density.

## 7. "What I do" line plus case story

**Line:** "I help [ICP in their words] [get specific outcome], [short how]."

> "I help iGaming marketing teams keep buying traffic at their old cost now that Meta bans got aggressive."

**Case story (20 to 30 seconds):** situation, what we did, concrete result, stop.

> "We just worked with a casino app around 150k users that had three ad accounts killed in a month. We put a layer in front of their Meta setup and they were back to roughly their old CPI within six weeks."

Then say nothing more. Let them ask.

## 8. Cold message structure and examples

**Structure (keep under about 80 words, shorter on messengers):**

1. Why *them* specifically. Something real: their talk, post, company news, a mutual.
2. The relevant observation or result. One line, no feature list.
3. A low-friction ask: a question, an opinion, a podcast invite. Not "30 min demo?"
4. An easy out.

**Example, value-first, messenger:**

> Hi Anna. Saw your talk at [event] on post-iOS attribution. The bit about blended CAC was sharp. I am recording a short podcast series with iGaming CMOs on what is replacing Meta for acquisition. Would you be up for a 25-minute conversation? Totally fine if not.

**Example, case-led, email:**

> Subject: Meta bans at [Company]
>
> Hi Mark. A few iGaming teams your size told me their Meta accounts are getting killed within weeks since the AI optimization rollout. We helped one (around 150k users) get back to near their old CPI in six weeks. Is this on your radar at [Company], or have you already solved it? Either answer is useful to me.

**Avoid:** long intros about yourself, attachments, fake familiarity, "just following up" chains, messaging on personal channels the person has not made public.

**Follow-up:** one nudge after 5 to 7 days with a new piece of value (a stat, an article, a relevant intro). Then stop.

## 9. Value-first formats

- Podcast or video interview series with the ICP
- Industry report or benchmark built from interviewing them, with the results shared back
- Private community, chat, or dinner series for peers
- Expert roundup article featuring their quotes
- Free audit or teardown of something public: their ads, onboarding, pricing page
- Introductions to people *they* want to meet
