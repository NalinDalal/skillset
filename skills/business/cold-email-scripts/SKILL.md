## The Offer Comes First

### The Copy Carries the Offer to the Prospect

If the offer is weak, better wording won't save it. Use a **frontend offer**.

### What Is a Frontend Offer?

A frontend offer is a small first step that gets your foot in the door. It sits in front of your main service or product.

**Examples:**
- If you run ads for companies: a free ad creative, or a free test campaign
- A low-ticket paid offer that leads naturally into the bigger offer
- Free logo mockup (real example: custom foodservice disposables manufacturer led with this, producing 1,061 leads in 70 days, 86% qualified in HubSpot, 8 deals closed)

### What It Does on the Call

The frontend offer pulls in qualified people who needed one extra reason to talk to you. They reply about the free thing, then on the call they often forget about it because they want the main service — but they're now qualified and ready to talk.

### When You Can't Use One

Some businesses have no small first step. Private equity is a good example — there's no trial version of buying a company. If that's you, skip it and lean harder on rules 2-4 (tailor to 1 industry/persona, tie offer to persona-level pain, use industry social proof).

### Tailor It to 1 Industry and 1 Persona

Write every email as if it's going to 1 person. That starts with the list.

**Never mix industries or personas inside a single list.** Each needs its own message.

**Example mistake:** Emailing CFOs about a marketing service. A CFO might sign off on the budget, but they're rarely the one deciding which marketing solution is best. The email reads as irrelevant and gets deleted.

### Tie the Offer to a Persona-Level Pain

Speak to the problem that specific person has right now.

**Example for a VP of Sales who just hired SDRs:**
> "Hey {{first_name}}, looks like you hired a few SDRs recently. I'm guessing you're in the 3 to 6 month ramp-up phase. We can set up a cold email system that sends lead flow to your team right away, so the ramp-up is less painful. Would you be interested in a free trial?"

This email talks about where they are. A generic "want a cold email campaign?" talks about you.

### Use Social Proof from Their Industry

Prospects have seen so many random case studies that most social proof barely registers now. The proof has to match their industry.

**Rule:** If you email car manufacturers, mention a car manufacturer. A software case study tells a car manufacturer you didn't look them up.

**Tested directly:** Emails with strong but unrelated social proof lost to the same emails with the proof removed.

**Rule of thumb:** Use proof from their own industry, or leave it out entirely.

### Use AI Personalization Only When It Sells the Offer

AI personalization is overhyped. Around 90% of our campaigns have no personalization at all. Our best campaign ever has zero.

**When the list is 1 persona in 1 industry segment,** the script is already written for them. The list becomes the message.

**When it does make sense:** When we offer outbound campaigns, we sometimes have AI write campaign ideas for each prospect's company. A template can't do that, and it helps them picture exactly what they'd get.

**Never use:** A line like "Saw your recent LinkedIn post about X" — in 2026, everyone can tell it was written by AI. Adds nothing.

## Making the Email Easy to Read

### Keep It Under 65 Words

Busy people skim. Under 65 words is the cap we've seen perform best. If you can get under 45, or even 35, even better.

**Reality check:** You'll see people get replies with 150-word emails, and those are outliers. Across millions of sends, shorter emails get read more, and emails that get read get more replies.

**A short limit also forces you to cut the fluff** and get to the point, which is what prospects want.

**Example:** An agency selling to 7-figure ecom brands kept every email between 15 and 30 words, put the offer first. Off 46,790 contacts, that brought in 176 qualified leads within 90 days.

### Write It Like a Text to a Coworker

The more formal or salesy it sounds, the more obvious it is that it's an automated cold email. Casual reads as 1 person writing to another.

**Aim for a 5th or 6th grade reading level.** They should understand the whole email in about 5 seconds.

**Rule:** A confused prospect says no.

### Keep the Subject Line 1 to 3 Words, Lowercase

The subject line matters the least out of everything in the email. Your only job is to avoid ruining it.

**A ruined subject line is 7 to 10 words long, with emojis, numbers, or spam words like "free."**

**Lowercase looks like it came from a coworker or a friend,** and it grabs more attention.

**Subject lines we use for an outbound offer:**
- `outbound`
- `outbound question`
- `{{company}} outbound`
- `outbound {{first_name}}?`

**For a SaaS product,** name the area you work in, like "marketing?"

### Make the Preview Text Do the Work

The preview text is what gets the email opened. The prospect reads it before they click. So the first line needs to stand out in the inbox.

**What works:** A specific pain point, and so does a relevant trigger.

**Favorite approach:** Putting the free offer right in the first line.
> "Hey {{first_name}}, would you be interested in a free X for {{company}}?"

**The full path:**
1. Lowercase subject line catches their eye
2. Preview text gets the open
3. Body builds enough trust to reply
4. Short CTA asks for the reply

### Spin Every Sentence

AI spam filters fingerprint email content. If they see the exact same email go out thousands of times, they flag it as spam. Your inboxes get burned and your IP reputation takes a hit.

**Spintax gives every sentence a few versions,** so the same email never goes out twice in the same form.

**Example:**
```
{Hey|Hi} {{first_name}}, {would you be interested in|open to} a free campaign for {{company}}?
```

**Copy fatigue:** For clients sending 100,000 to 500,000 emails a month, even spun copy wears out. The replies drop out of nowhere and it looks like an inbox problem. Usually it's the filters catching on to the same framework. Without spintax, it happens within a few days.

**Keep writing and testing new copy the whole time.**

## The 5 Scripts We Run

These are the 5 frameworks that keep winning across 10M+ sends. Each one is the template first, then a real example.

### 1. Lead Magnet + Case Study

**Template:**
> "{{first_name}}, created a [lead magnet] for {{company}} covering the strategy we used to help [client] get [result] in [time frame]. Mind if I share it?"

**Use when:** You have a strong, recognizable case study, or a lead magnet you can tie back to one.

**Opens with proof and a free asset.** The meeting ask comes after they've seen it and trust you.

**Example, from a TikTok Shop offer:**
> "John, created a TikTok Shop playbook for {{company}} outlining the exact strategy we used to launch [client] on TikTok's platform and scale their shop from $0 to $200K a month in 90 days (influencers feature the products on a fully commissioned basis). Mind if I send it over?"

**The playbook is the case study,** packaged as something they can keep. The note in brackets adds one more reason it's worth reading.

---

### 2. One-Line Free Offer

**Template:**
> "{{first_name}}, interested in [free work or front-end offer] for {{company}}?
> PS: [social proof]"

**This is one of the best-performing emails** I've ever sent. It's 1 line, and the PS is optional. The whole email runs on a no-brainer frontend offer.

**Example:**
> "Hey Sarah, interested in a free cold email campaign where we target 10,000 ICP prospects for Acme Corp?"

**Why it works:** They read it and think, "Great offer, but who are these people?" So they check your website, see you're legit, and reply. On the call, a lot of them skip the free work and ask about working with us.

**We've run this for a lot of clients over the last 3 years,** and it keeps working.

---

### 3. Direct Offer + Guarantee

**Template:**
> "{{first_name}}, interested in [dream result] for {{company}} in the next [time frame]? Asking because our [mechanism] guarantees [result] or [risk reversal]. Open to it?"

**Use when:** Your main offer is strong enough on its own and you can back it with a real guarantee.

**You skip the free trial** because the guarantee does the job.

**Example:**
> "Hey Sarah, would you be interested in generating 1 million views for your brand in the next 30 days? Asking since our influencer campaigns guarantee 1 million views in 30 days or we refund you. Worth a conversation?"

**This one has worked well for TikTok Shop, TikTok in general and Instagram offers.** There's far more upside than downside for the prospect, and the time frame is short.

---

### 4. Pain Point + Solution

**Template:**
> "{{first_name}}, [question about a real pain point]? Our [solution] helps fix [problem], so you get [end result]. Worth a look?
> PS: [social proof]"

**Use when:** You have a specific pain point the market feels.

**The trap:** "Are you struggling with pipeline?" is too broad. Every company would say yes, so it means nothing. The pain has to be specific enough that nobody else is emailing about it.

**Example, for a client selling smart glasses to roofers (synced to the client's CRM):**
> "Hey Tyler, how are your crews handling roofing expenses right now? Phone in one hand, clipboard in the other? We developed smart glasses for roofers that let your team capture photos, pull up checklists, and document damage completely hands-free while staying on the roof. Mind if I share how they work?"

**This one did amazing.** The roofer had probably never thought of that as a problem. It was just how the job worked, until the email described it.

**PS: Add social proof from their industry.**

---

### 5. Relevant Touchpoint + Observed Issue

**Template:**
> "{{first_name}}, [relevant touchpoint] and noticed [specific issue you scraped]. Interested in a quick fix?"

**This one takes more research,** because you need a signal that's harder to find. You scrape a weakness you can fix, ideally with a frontend offer.

**Examples of weaknesses you can fix:**
- A slow website
- A missing demo video on the homepage
- A missing welcome flow on email list

**Example, for an email marketing offer:**
> "Hey {{first_name}}, signed up to your email list last week and noticed you don't have a welcome flow set up. I never got a single email after subscribing. Interested in a free welcome flow build out?"

**You can add proof from another brand** where you set this up and grew their email revenue.

**When it's done right, this is one of the strongest emails** you can send.

## Which Script to Use

Pick the script based on what you have to work with. Match the script to your assets:

| If you have... | Use this script |
|---|---|
| A strong case study | Lead magnet + case study |
| A free or low-ticket first step | 1 line free offer |
| A strong offer and a real guarantee | Direct offer + guarantee |
| A specific pain only your market feels | Pain point + solution |
| A signal you can scrape and fix | Relevant touchpoint + observed issue |

**Most clients have 2 or 3 of these.** So we write a version of each one that fits and let the replies decide.

## How to Write Your Own Script

### Step 1: Lock the List
1 industry segment and 1 persona per list. Everything else in the script depends on knowing exactly who reads it.

### Step 2: Pick the Offer
Decide on the frontend offer, or on the guarantee if you're going direct. Tie it to a pain that this persona has right now.

### Step 3: Pick the Framework
Use the table from the last section (match your assets to the 5 frameworks).

### Step 4: Write the First Line
The first line is your preview text, so write it before anything else. Put the free offer, the specific pain, or the observed issue right there.

### Step 5: Cut It Down
Get it under 65 words. Then try for 45. Swap every complicated word for a simple one.

### Step 6: Score It
Run it against the 10-point checklist. If it hits fewer than 8, fix it before it goes out.

### Step 7: Spin It and Test It
Add spintax to every sentence. Run versions side by side, then put more volume behind the one that books more meetings.

### Step 8: Refresh It
When the replies drop and your inboxes check out, assume copy fatigue. Write the next version from the same framework with new wording.

### One Last Thing
Even a perfect script gets nothing if it lands in spam. Plenty of B2B companies are landing in spam right now and have no idea. Check your deliverability before you blame the copy.

## 10-Point Checklist: Quick Scoring

After writing your script, score it out of 10. You need **at least 8** to go live.

| Score | If Missing... |
|---|---|
| **Hit 8-10** | Go live |
| **Hit 6-7** | Fix the weakest point, then go live |
| **Hit fewer than 6** | Rewrite from scratch using the framework that best matches your assets |

**Each missing point is a fixable improvement.** Prioritize: frontend offer + persona tailoring + word count first, then social proof + spintax.
