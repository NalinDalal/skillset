## 1. The 7 outbound post types

Ranked by how directly each one produces pipeline. Start at the top.

### 1. The blueprint

Share a result, then invite people to comment a keyword to get the playbook behind it. Send the resource by DM, with a clear path to book a demo.

The result gets attention. The playbook builds interest. The comment keyword does the qualifying for you.

### 2. The direct results post

Share a compelling result. Partnering with creators on these works well.

"Want to know which tool made it happen? Comment to find out."

Then send people to a simple landing page where they book a demo directly.

### 3. Founder-led content

Share what is happening inside the company: decisions, challenges, lessons, and wins. Give people a reason to relate to you and follow the journey.

This works especially well when other founders are part of the ideal customer profile. Founders read other founders' build logs.

### 4. Value-first content

Skip the gated resource. Share the full breakdown in the post: what you tried, what worked, and how someone else can apply it.

These posts often get fewer interactions and convert extremely well. Fewer people means a higher-intent audience.

### 5. Customer case studies

Let the customer take the spotlight. Film them. Break down their workflows. Explain what they achieved and why it worked. Show prospects what success looks like for them.

### 6. Product and company announcements

New features, integrations, funding announcements. Use these to show what is changing and why it matters to the audience.

### 7. Event posts

Webinars, in-person events, live sessions. Give people a clear reason to attend and a simple way to sign up.

## 2. The 14 client-getting content pillars

The raw material list. Every post you write maps to one of these.

1. Your origin story
2. The problem you solve
3. Who you are for
4. Who you are not for
5. The mistakes buyers make
6. Your unique point of view
7. Your unique framework
8. Your client results
9. Typical customer objections
10. A live demo
11. A live demonstration of expertise
12. What working with you looks like
13. Why your approach is different
14. Your clear offer

Pillars 3 and 4 do the most work and get the least credit. "Who this is not for" filters the pipeline before sales ever touches it.

## 3. Rules that make posts land

### Give people a reason to trust you

When a stranger messages, they check your profile first. Make sure it explains who you are, what you are building, and who it helps. An empty profile kills the message before it is read.

### Make the first message easy to answer

"Would you be open to exploring a partnership?" gets no reply. "Saw you are hiring five engineers. How are you finding candidates?" gets one.

Open questions about their situation get replies. Questions about your offer do not.

### Do not automate yet

The engineer in every founder wants to automate posting and DMs. Do not, yet. First learn who cares about the problem and which messages hit the pain point. Automating before you know what works just scales the wrong message.

### Talk about the best thing you know

Impart real knowledge. Show how to do the work well. Drop the gyaan-bait posts on LinkedIn and X, they reach nobody. Talk about the actual problem, and about building the thing.

### Repost the best replies

Turn the sharpest comment or thread reply into a standalone post. It converts and it compounds.
