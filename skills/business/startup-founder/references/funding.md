# Funding: Bootstrap vs Raise

## Decision framework
Raise money only if BOTH are true:
1. The opportunity requires spending significantly ahead of revenue. Examples: building a marketplace that needs supply and demand simultaneously, capital-intensive hardware, land-grab network effects where speed beats profitability.
2. You are willing to trade equity + control + a board for that speed.

Bootstrap if:
- You can reach paying customers within weeks/months with a small team.
- The market rewards profitability and steady growth over blitzscaling (most dev tools, agencies, niche SaaS, service businesses).
- You want to keep full control and optionality. You can always raise later once there is traction. This gives much better terms.

Nerdev-style studio/SaaS profile for Nalin: bootstrapping is the default fit. Raise only if a specific product idea genuinely needs upfront capital to win. This is rare for solo or small dev-tooling plays.

## If bootstrapping
- Revenue-fund from day one: charge early, even a small amount, to validate willingness to pay.
- Consider services/freelancing (see the `freelancing` skill if available) to fund product development while building the real product on the side.
- Use startup credit programs to cut costs. Programs: AWS Activate, Google Cloud for Startups, Microsoft for Startups, MongoDB for Startups, and the Notion/Segment/Stripe Atlas programs. Apply for these once incorporated. They cut infra cost close to zero for the first year.

## If raising
### Sources
- Pre-seed/seed: angel investors (personal networks, AngelList, Indian angel networks: Indian Angel Network, LetsVenture, Venture Catalysts). Accelerators: Y Combinator, 500 Startups/500 Global, Techstars. In India: Startup India Seed Fund and various state-level programs.
- The YC guide (yc/library "A Guide to Seed Fundraising") is the single best free resource. Read it before pitching anyone.

### Pitch deck (standard structure, ~10-12 slides)
1. Problem
2. Solution
3. Why now
4. Market size (bottom-up, not just "huge TAM")
5. Product (demo/screenshots)
6. Traction (any signal: users, revenue, LOIs, waitlist)
7. Business model / pricing
8. Go-to-market
9. Competition / why you win
10. Team
11. Financials/ask (how much, for what, runway)
12. Contact/close

### What investors actually check
- Founder-market fit: why *you* are positioned to win this.
- Evidence you talked to real customers (not just an idea in a vacuum).
- Clear, specific use of funds and milestones the round buys.

## India-specific funding notes
- **Startup India** registration (DPIIT recognition) gives tax benefits, easier compliance, and access to the Startup India Seed Fund Scheme. Do this once you have Pvt Ltd registration and reasonable traction, not before.
- Indian angel networks: Indian Angel Network, LetsVenture, Venture Catalysts, 100X.VC, Titan Capital.
- Razorpay, MongoDB, AWS, and several others run India-specific startup programs (free credits, discounted tooling). Check current terms because they change. Search rather than rely on memory.
- FDI/foreign investment rules matter if raising from outside India. This needs a CA/lawyer, not general advice.
