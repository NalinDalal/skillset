## 1. The 6 levels

Same ladder as AARRR (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral), written as operating steps.

### Level 1: traffic generation

Drive as many of the right people to your brand as you can. Four channels:

- **Content.** LinkedIn, X, YouTube, SEO. See `content-playbook.md`.
- **Ads.** Google, LinkedIn, Meta, Reddit. Only after paying users exist.
- **Outbound.** Cold email, LinkedIn DMs, cold calls, warm intros. See `decision-maker-outreach`.
- **Partnerships.** Agency programs, affiliates, referrals, integrations.

### Level 2: lead capture

Turn that attention into a contact you own:

- Lead magnets
- Webinars
- Newsletter
- Community
- Social engagement
- Product signups

The test for this level: can you email the person tomorrow without asking permission?

### Level 3: lead nurture

Keep them warm:

- Email flows
- Retargeting ads
- SDR touchpoints
- Educational content

### Level 4: conversion

Five ways to turn a visitor into an application:

- Case studies
- Testimonials
- Free tools
- Resources
- Video demos

### Level 5: qualification

- Auto-enrich and qualify every submission with Clay, HubSpot, RevenueHero, or Default.
- Nurture approved leads 9 or more times before they ever talk to an AE.
- Send disqualified leads an email and cancel the call.

### Level 6: retention and expansion

- Keep and grow accounts through recurring services, courses, affiliates, and referrals.
- Land and expand is one of the best enterprise strategies. The first logo is the hardest sale; the second product is the cheapest.

## 3. Getting the first 100 users

You do not need to be a genius, only relentless. Six steps:

1. **Launch everywhere.** Product Hunt, DevHunt, BetaList, Peerlist, AppSumo, Indie Hackers. If it lets you list your product, list it.
2. **Post on socials like your life depends on it.** One post will not do it. Do it 100 days in a row. Copy what went viral, tweak, repeat.
3. **Stalk your competitors.** See where they are listed, then submit your product in the same places.
4. **Pair AI output with SEO.** Spin up posts with an LLM, then edit every line. Fifty solid posts move mountains. Get domain rating to 15 or higher.
5. **Run some ads.** Google, Facebook, even Bing. Optimize once, then let it run.
6. **Send cold DMs and replies.** Find your people. Be short, be real, be helpful. One sentence pitch. No spam.

This is how the internet is won. No secret, just consistent boring work.

## 4. What actually produced the first 100

The honest version, from a founder with no ads and no blog. Ads and blogs only start working after some users are already paying. Before that, 90% of the job is talking to people who already have the problem.

1. **DM people who had already complained about the exact problem** on LinkedIn and X. Keep it short and simple.
2. **Reply under every post in the niche for 30 days.** Not "check out my tool." Real answers with real insight.
3. **Obsess over the first 20 users.** Calls, Loom videos, or fixing their setup by hand.
4. **Post the money numbers.** $0, then $10k. People trust authenticity.

## 5. The weekly growth target habit

Write the weekly growth target before Monday, then treat missing it as the only failure that week.

- Pick revenue, or real active users if you do not charge yet.
- Use the same definition as last week. Changing the metric mid-quarter is cheating.
- If you miss, change the product or the user. Never rewrite the target.

Put the number on the wall this Sunday. Measure the result by Friday. If you missed, fix something. Do not explain it away.

The goal is not to predict growth. It is to build a company that keeps hitting the number.

## 6. YC sales advice

- Sales is dating: lead, nurture, close.
- Listen 70%, talk 30%.
- It is a number game.
- Authenticity is your best sales asset.

As a founder you will always be your company's best salesperson. Sales skills compound with every conversation you have.

## 7. GTM patterns from 25 YC companies

Most of these cost $0. The pattern underneath them: do things that do not scale, own one painful problem, make the product the marketing.

| # | Company | What they did |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Airbnb | Built a "post to Craigslist" feature to take supply from a bigger platform |
| 2 | Dropbox | A 3-minute demo video on Hacker News built a huge waitlist before launch. Gave free storage for referrals on both sides |
| 3 | Stripe | "Collision install": set up Stripe on your laptop on the spot |
| 4 | Reddit | Founders posted from many accounts so the site never looked empty |
| 5 | DoorDash | Started as a basic website with a phone number, founders delivered the food themselves |
| 6 | Instacart | Founders did the grocery runs themselves to learn the whole process |

| 9 | PostHog | Open source plus very honest, funny marketing that developers actually share |
| 10 | Supabase | "Open-source Firebase alternative": one line that explains everything |
| 11 | GitLab | Built in public, with an open handbook and a public roadmap |
| 12 | Twitch | Started as one guy streaming his life 24/7 |
| 13 | Gusto | Wanted customers so badly they did payroll by hand at first |
| 14 | Coinbase | Made buying crypto as easy as a bank app when everything else was hard |
| 15 | Brex | Started with one sharp group: funded startups that banks ignored |
| 16 | Deel | Solved one painful problem: paying contractors around the world |
| 17 | Razorpay | Made developer docs and setup easy for Indian startups |
| 18 | Meesho | Grew through WhatsApp sellers instead of ads |
| 19 | Retool | Free for small teams, and they grew inside companies from there |
| 20 | Heroku | Deploying with one command was the marketing |
| 21 | Mixpanel | Sold to the developers who would actually use it |
| 22 | Weebly | A free plan that put "Powered by Weebly" on every site |
| 23 | Twitch (again) | Paid gamers to stream before there was an audience |
| 24 | Scale AI | Did unscalable, manual work for the first big AI labs |
| 25 | PagerDuty | Went after one painful moment: the 3am outage |
