# Infrastructure

Source note: the Za-zu Cold Email Handbook covers this ground in depth. These are the additions and the short version. Do this LAST: the first 50 emails go out of a personal inbox with none of this.

## Start small

Begin with 1 to 2 domains and 3 to 5 inboxes, sending 20 to 40 emails per day total. Bad messaging plus big infrastructure means burning the lead list faster. Authentication problems and warmup mistakes are easier to catch on a small batch.

## Domains

Never send from the primary company domain. Buy lookalikes at around $10 each: for acme.com, try tryacme.com, acmehq.com, getacme.com.

Capacity formula: domains needed = (daily volume / 60) x 1.1. Each domain holds 3 inboxes at max 20 emails per inbox per day.

## DNS records

Without authentication, mail goes straight to spam.

- **SPF:** v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all
- **DKIM:** generated by your mail provider, added at DNS as a TXT entry; proves the message is untampered and authorized.
- **DMARC:** start at v=DMARC1; p=none; to monitor, move to p=quarantine once pass rates look clean.

DNS lookup scripts can verify all three records for you in bulk.

## Warmup

New domains have no reputation. Warm 2 to 4 weeks: automated open-bait exchanges with real inbox activity plus replies, building trust at Gmail and Outlook before any real send.

## Google Postmaster Tools

Free, 10 minutes to set up. Add every sending domain, verify with a DNS TXT record, then check daily. The dashboard reports spam rate, domain reputation tier, authentication pass rates, and delivery errors. On a drop to Low or Bad reputation, stop sending immediately and diagnose. Turn on the reputation email alerts.

## Reply handling at scale

Replies scattered across many inboxes and domains get lost. Aggregate them into one unified inbox (any multi-account Gmail aggregator works).

## Scaling up

Once 2 or more weeks pass with clean deliverability and steady replies:

- Add domains in batches of 3 to 5; warm each batch before sending from it.
- Rotate domains on a 90-day cycle; keep the next batch warming in the background.
- Watch Postmaster for each domain. Similar naming patterns across domains mean one bad domain can drag the others.
