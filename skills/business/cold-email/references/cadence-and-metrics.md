# Cadence, Metrics, and Iteration

## The blitz cadence

Email 1, email 2 the next day, email 3 two days later, email 4 the day after. The full sequence lands in 4 to 5 days. Conventional playbooks stretch it over 2 to 4 weeks. The source runs blitz and the reasoning applies to any early-stage founder:

1. **Signal fast.** A 4-week cadence delays the messaging verdict by a month. A blitz answers by Friday.
2. **Speed forces a response.** Compressed timing gets more total replies. Early on, a "not interested" proves the lead saw the sequence. Silence over a month proves nothing.
3. **Spam is a content problem.** Prospects flag emails when they are lazy and irrelevant, not when a follow-up lands in 24 hours. The blitz only works when scripting and personalization were done first.
4. **Multi-channel density.** If LinkedIn calls or cold calls run in parallel, a compressed cadence makes every channel land in the same week. Familiarity, not scattered pings.

## Send 7 days a week

Weekend sending costs nothing measurable for founder and startup ICPs, and it keeps you off the days everyone else piles on. Exception: enterprise procurement and similar gatekeeper ICPs read on weekdays only.

## Spread sends across the day

Randomize delivery across a window like 7 AM to 5 PM in prospect-local time. Two hundred emails at 8:01 AM from one domain is a detectable pattern. More sends beat tighter timing precision.

## Track daily

- **Deliverability.** Track it first; nothing else matters when it drops.
- **Reply rate**, positive and negative. Read every reply by hand.
- **Bounce rate.** Stay under 2 percent. High bounces damage sender reputation.
- **Spam complaint rate.** Gmail filters a domain at 0.3 percent. Stay under 0.1.

## Update signals continuously

Feed verdicts back into the filter rules. A company size that never replies becomes a cut. A segment that triples reply rate gets weight. Negatives are filtering data.

## Run parallel campaigns

Keep 2 to 3 live with different angles against the same ICP. Judge a campaign after 50 to 100 sends; pause the loser, move volume to the winner.

## Turn off open tracking at volume

At high volume, turn open tracking off. The pixel itself is a filter target, and Apple Mail proxies pre-load pixels regardless of reading, plus corporate-security scanners open everything. Open rates carry no signal. Keep it on only below roughly 50 emails per day.

## Sync replies to a CRM

One record per company and contact across campaigns, so replies line up and nobody gets double-touched. Attio and HubSpot both work.
