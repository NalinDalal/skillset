# The 4-Email Sequence

Write it by hand. Budget 3 hours for a sequence that runs 4 weeks. Pick one of your most disappointed users and write every line the way they talk.

## Email 1

Four parts, in this order: hook, value, CTA, subject.

### Hook

Two shapes:

- **Offer hook.** Trade something tangible for their time.

  "Hi Jordan, I'd love to buy you a meal at Ssam Bar in exchange for your time (yes this is a cold email)."

- **Value hook.** Promise one measurable outcome in one line.

  "Hi Jordan, I promise I can replace your VA in the Philippines for $30."

Both should sound like cold emails. The prospect already knows it is one. Lean into it.

Offer hooks fit high-ACV sales and ICP validation, when volume matters and one meeting is worth thousands. Keep the offer at $25 scale, not $200 scale. Value hooks fit proven messaging, when reply quality matters more than reply count. An offer hook adds volume; a value hook filters harder but lifts reply quality.

The hook is preview text. Subject plus hook is everything the prospect sees before opening, and the hook is the first thing they read before archiving. If it does not earn the next sentence, stop there.

### Value

State the differentiator. Keep it short.

"We're building an intelligent inbox for sales teams, and to make it actually useful we want to learn how growth people handle email today. What tools you're stitching together, where things slip through, what you wish existed."

This one trades on a differentiator that is not a feature: the prospect believes the company innovates faster than incumbents, and the question lets them feel it.

### CTA

Make it a yes-no question. Every extra decision step loses replies.

"Would you be down for a 15 min call? Tuesday or Thursday next week?"

A yes plus one weekday is enough to book the meeting.

### Subject

Do not innovate. Copy subjects that work in other campaigns.

"Maya, Tatte gift card for 15 min"

"re: your outbound workflow"

Never lie in a subject. False "re:" framing also breaks federal cold email rules.

## Email 2

Reply on email 1's thread. Send it the next day. Do not open a new thread: a prospect who opens email 2 almost certainly saw email 1, so a reset buries the context and reads like a bot.

Short reply shape: one line of new value, nothing re-introduced.

"Hey, sorry if I misjudged your taste, would a Chipotle gift card work better? PS: You can also text Slashy from iMessage mid-meeting and it'll pull up the prospect's LinkedIn, your full email history, and draft your follow-up before you hang up."

No signature. It should look like a person, not a campaign.

## Email 3

Two days after email 2. The first approach failed, so switch angles and pull a different differentiator. Case studies land well here; a prospect still reading is on the edge, and proof tips them.

"Figured I'd skip the bribery this time.

Most growth people I talk to have the same problem. A prospect opens your email 3 times, but you don't see it until 2 days later when they've already gone cold. Or you crush a demo but the follow-up sits in drafts for a week.

Slashy catches both. Open alerts hit your phone in real time, and when you hang up from a call, your follow-up's already written in your voice.

Worth 10 min? No more gift card bribes, promise."

## Email 4

The breakup email. Unlike 2 and 3, it opens a NEW thread with a new subject. Reasons: a fresh subject earns a fresh open attempt, and a fourth "Re:" with no new information reads as spam.

Subjects signal finality, not another pitch: "closing the loop", "last one from me".

"Hi Maya,

Last one from me, I promise.

If you're already on top of every follow-up and never miss an open, genuinely impressed. Every prospect gets a timely reply, every demo gets a same-day follow-up, nothing goes cold. Hard to improve on that.

Either way, curious what's working for you.

Best, Harsha, COO @ Slashy"

Three moves:

1. Apologize for the frequency. Disarms the defense.
2. Fake-compliment the status quo. Each clause is a pain point framed as an achievement. If it is not true, they book the call.
3. Ego-bait CTA. "Curious what's working for you" makes the ask a favor. No day pair, no hard close.

Bring back the full signature here only.

## Structure summary

| Aspect | Email 2 / 3 | Email 4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thread | Reply on email 1 | New thread, new subject |
| Signature | None | Full signature |
| Tone | Bump / pitch reload | Permission to walk away |
| CTA | Day pair | Soft "swap notes" ask |

Email 4 is typically the highest-reply-rate email in the sequence.
