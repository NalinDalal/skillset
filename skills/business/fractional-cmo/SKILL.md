# Fractional CMO

A senior marketing leader working part-time across the whole business, rather than an executor producing assets. Covers both sides of the engagement: someone deciding whether to move into the role, and someone deciding whether to buy one.

## The problem this role solves

Many brands do not have a marketing problem. They have too many people doing marketing and nobody owning it.

- A performance agency runs ads.
- A social team posts.
- A PR person does outreach.
- A designer makes creative.
- Maybe there is an in-house team too.

Everyone is working. Nobody is asking the bigger questions:

- What do we prioritise?
- Where does the budget go?
- What do we stop doing?
- What is the brand known for?
- How does any of this show up in revenue?

That gap is the whole reason the fractional CMO model works. These companies do not need another executor. They need someone who connects the moving parts and makes sharper calls.

## What the role covers

A real fractional CMO thinks across the business, not inside one channel:

- Positioning
- Growth strategy
- Budgets and forecasting
- Channel mix
- Team direction
- Agency management
- Sales alignment
- How marketing connects to actual revenue

The deliverable is decisions, not output. If the engagement produces more content and no changed allocation, it failed.

## Who needs one, who does not

**Needs a fractional CMO:**
- Revenue exists but marketing is a cost centre nobody can tie to growth.
- Four or more vendors, no one reconciling them.
- The founder or CEO makes every marketing call because nobody else can.
- A full-time CMO is unaffordable or not yet justified by stage.
- The team executes well and still misses the same targets.

**Does not need one yet:**
- Nobody has product-market fit. Fix that first.
- One channel, one founder, no team. Do the work directly.
- The real problem is a weak product or a broken offer. Better marketing surfaces that, it does not fix it.
- The company needs an executor, not a decision maker. Hire the executor.

## The skill gap

Changing a LinkedIn headline tomorrow does not make anyone a CMO. The role needs depth well past marketing execution:

- P&L and margins
- Unit economics
- Budgets and forecasting
- Sales
- Customer behaviour
- Team management
- Channel prioritisation
- Decision-making when there is no perfect answer

The higher you go in marketing, the less the job becomes doing everything yourself. It becomes knowing what should be done, what should not, and why.

## If you are moving into the role

Your execution background matters less than the gap analysis above. Audit yourself honestly against the skill list and name which items you cannot yet do.

Typical bridge for a marketer who came up through content, performance, or brand:

1. Get comfortable reading a P&L and building a forecast, even a simple one.
2. Learn unit economics for the client's model: CAC, LTV, payback period, gross margin.
3. Practice channel prioritisation under a fixed budget, where something must lose.
4. Get reps managing people and vendors, since the role directs a team rather than doing the work.

Do the bridge as real work on a real client or your own business. A course is not the same.

## Positioning

Position on the decisions you make, not on the channels you know. "Fractional CMO" alone is a label anyone can claim.

Stronger framings:

- "I decide where your marketing budget goes, and I can tell you which channel to stop funding."
- "I connect marketing spend to the revenue number your board asks about."
- "For companies with four agencies and no one reconciling them."

Weak framings:

- "Fractional CMO with 10 years of experience."
- "Full-service marketing strategy."
- Anything listing channels you can run.

## Pricing the engagement

Structure the retainer around scope and decision rights, not hours. See the `pricing` skill for the mechanics of value-based pricing.

The commercial questions to settle in writing before starting:

- How many hours per week, and who decides when they are spent.
- Which decisions the CMO owns outright, and which need client sign-off.
- What access they get to financials, sales data, and product roadmap.
- The review cadence and what happens at it.
- The notice period on both sides.
- Whether there is a one-off strategy phase priced separately from the ongoing retainer.

Splitting an initial strategy phase from the ongoing retainer matters. The strategy phase has a finish line, and charging the same rate for both makes the client feel the retainer is padding.

## Guardrails

- Do not promise revenue. Promise decisions, clarity, and accountability for spend.
- Do not run the channels yourself. That recreates the executor problem the role exists to solve.
- Get access to the numbers before the engagement starts. A CMO without financials is a content strategist with a title.
- If the client will not let you cut anything, say the engagement cannot work. Agreeing to fund everything is agreeing to decide nothing.
